# Marketing practice curriculum

Research reviewed 9 September 2026. 17 new trees; 131 new practice nodes. With the existing Copywriting and Ad creation foundations: 19 trees and 154 nodes.

## How to work through it

Start in Library → Skills → Marketing → Marketing foundations. Do one foundation exercise today. Use one real business for early practice and a second for transfer. Keep an evidence folder containing original work, source material, feedback and revisions. Paste an artifact link or enough of the output to assess into each practice session note. The app stores your time, note and self-rating; it does not automatically inspect external artifacts or certify their quality.

This is an original curriculum informed by [Justin Skycak’s Advice on Upskilling](https://www.justinmath.com/advice-on-upskilling/): observable skills, prerequisites, active retrieval, error correction, spaced review and varied practice. The marketing exercises and rubrics are our application of that method, not Skycak’s own marketing syllabus. No source establishes a uniquely best learning method for every marketing specialty.

Begin with a manageable daily block, for example 25 minutes: five minutes of a due review, fifteen producing or analysing something, five comparing it with the rubric and correcting one error. This schedule is a starting suggestion, not a scientifically optimal ratio. Read the relevant resource only when you need instruction for the next drill. Track reading as Study and active production, analysis, retrieval or correction as Practice. Pause for interruptions. Larger projects can span several sessions.

On a miss, identify the missing prerequisite or error. Consult a worked example or reviewer, correct the work, and mark the attempt helped. Later attempt a fresh case without the solution. For clean repetitions use the node’s variation, new evidence or changed constraints. Return on the app’s due reviews. Add a delayed fresh-context repetition after about a week and mix previously learned problem types once you can perform them separately. These are practical defaults; adapt the interval to actual forgetting and performance.

Do not train every branch at once. First establish foundations and research, then choose a production lane: Copywriting → Copywriting mastery, or Ad creation → Creative strategy & production. Learn analytics before interpreting campaign tests. Add one acquisition or retention channel relevant to your business. Leadership integrates the portfolio at the end. Preparation listed below is recommended across trees; only the prerequisite edges inside each tree are automatically enforced.

## Assessment and progression

A saved timer session proves time was logged, not that the work was good. Mark clean only when every listed criterion is met independently. Use helped when you needed a model answer, coaching or a correction, and missed when you cannot yet perform it. The app’s reliable/mastered labels are estimates from self-reported practice evidence, not external credentials. A same-session correction is useful practice but not independent retrieval.

For creative work, ask a relevant reader to explain the message or complete the task, then ask a practitioner to critique craft and reasoning. For numerical work, compare with the worked answer and independently reproduce calculations. For research, preserve source links and disconfirming cases. For operations, inject failures. For strategy, defend your assumptions to a business owner and later compare your forecasts with outcomes. Small qualitative pilots find problems; they do not establish market prevalence or conversion lift.

At capstones, obtain external critique and preserve the before/after work. Use a simple review rubric: 0 = absent or wrong, 1 = usable with correction, 2 = independent and evidenced. Score accuracy, customer relevance, execution and decision reasoning separately; revise every zero. Do not convert this local rubric into a claimed industry percentile.

“Top 0.001%” is an ambition. Demonstrated excellence requires repeated results, strong judgement and independent challenge across real business contexts. Where a field test is unavailable, label simulations and leave external market validation outstanding. No time target or completed checklist guarantees elite performance.

## Practice chapters

### Marketing foundations

Start here. Practice method, customer value, arithmetic, evidence and a first marketing diagnosis.

Preparation: None; use the entry exercise.

- [MIT OpenCourseWare · Marketing Management](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/15-810-marketing-management-fall-2010/pages/syllabus/) — Value → diagnosis: use the syllabus and lecture notes to distinguish customer, product, price, promotion and distribution decisions. Reconstruct your own case after reading.

#### Set up a practice loop

Prerequisites: Entry skill.

Train an observable ability, compare with a standard, correct the weakest part, then attempt a fresh case. Reading time and direct practice are separate.

**Practice.** Choose one real business and a second business for transfer. Spend 15 minutes writing a customer brief without AI or notes; compare it with customer evidence. Log the draft, error and correction using the practice timer.

Next repetition: Tomorrow reconstruct the brief unaided. Use due reviews in the app; after a clean repetition, change the business. Start with a daily 25-minute block: 5 review, 15 production, 5 feedback.

**Standard.** Brief names customer, buying situation, alternative, offer and action. Evidence is separate from assumptions; only active work is logged as practice.

Meet every criterion independently before rating clean. Log your output and corrections.

#### Map a customer decision

Prerequisites: Set up a practice loop.

Marketing connects customer needs with product, price, distribution and communication. An ad cannot repair every business constraint.

**Practice.** Map one recent purchase: trigger, alternatives, evaluation, purchase, use and repeat. Attach an observation to each stage and identify which marketing decision could change it.

Next repetition: Map a service purchase, then a B2B purchase involving several people.

**Standard.** Six stages grounded in behaviour; at least one product, price, access and communication decision; no invented motives.

Meet every criterion independently before rating clean. Log your output and corrections.

#### Separate facts from hypotheses

Prerequisites: Set up a practice loop.

A claim needs a traceable source. Association, customer opinion and a randomised comparison support different conclusions.

**Practice.** Collect ten marketing claims. Label each observation, interpretation or causal claim. For each causal claim name a competing explanation and the evidence needed to distinguish it.

Next repetition: Use ten unfamiliar claims, including one impressive case study with missing denominators.

**Standard.** All sources recorded; no before/after chart described as causal proof; three claims rewritten at the strength their evidence supports.

Meet every criterion independently before rating clean. Log your output and corrections.

#### Calculate funnel rates

Prerequisites: Map a customer decision.

A rate divides outcomes by the relevant opportunities. Percentage-point change is different from relative change.

**Practice.** From 20,000 impressions, 400 visits, 40 leads and 8 sales, calculate each stage rate and total sale rate. Compare a lead rate rising from 10% to 12%. Work by hand, then check in a sheet.

Next repetition: Generate five new funnels; include a zero denominator and explain why that rate is undefined.

**Standard.** Answers: 2%, 10%, 20%, 0.04%; increase is 2 percentage points or 20% relative. Every rate labels its denominator.

Meet every criterion independently before rating clean. Log your output and corrections.

#### Calculate contribution and CAC

Prerequisites: Calculate funnel rates.

Revenue is not profit. Contribution subtracts variable costs; acquisition cost must specify which costs and which new customers it includes.

**Practice.** Model 100 orders at $100, $40 variable cost per order and $2,000 acquisition spend for 50 new customers. Calculate revenue, pre-ad contribution, contribution after ads, paid CAC and ROAS.

Next repetition: Add refunds and repeat purchases. Reconcile paid CAC with fully loaded blended CAC.

**Standard.** Answers: $10,000, $6,000, $4,000, $40 and 5x. Fixed costs, refunds and organic acquisition costs are explicitly excluded rather than forgotten.

Meet every criterion independently before rating clean. Log your output and corrections.

#### Build a metric tree

Prerequisites: Calculate funnel rates; Calculate contribution and CAC.

Connect a business outcome to controllable inputs; guardrails prevent improving a proxy while damaging the business.

**Practice.** Draw revenue as customers × orders per customer × net order value. Add contribution costs. Choose one outcome, two input metrics and two guardrails for the business.

Next repetition: Build the equivalent tree for a subscription or a sales-led service.

**Standard.** Units reconcile; inputs can be acted on; a scenario shows clicks increasing while profit falls.

Meet every criterion independently before rating clean. Log your output and corrections.

#### Find the binding constraint

Prerequisites: Separate facts from hypotheses; Build a metric tree.

Diagnose before selecting a channel. Low demand, weak offer, inaccessible distribution and poor conversion need different interventions.

**Practice.** Write a one-page diagnosis using five facts, three competing explanations and one discriminating next test. Rank actions by expected benefit, confidence and effort with stated assumptions.

Next repetition: Use a business with high conversion but little reach, then one with traffic and no sales.

**Standard.** Recommendation addresses an evidenced constraint; a rival explanation survives; test could change the decision.

Meet every criterion independently before rating clean. Log your output and corrections.

#### Defend a marketing diagnosis

Prerequisites: Find the binding constraint.

Transfer requires a fresh business and external challenge, not a polished retelling of your original case.

**Practice.** Analyse your second business without copying the first brief. Give a practitioner or business owner your metric tree, evidence and proposed experiment. Record their strongest objection and revise.

Next repetition: After a week, diagnose a third category in 45 minutes and compare errors against your first attempt.

**Standard.** Arithmetic reconciles; reviewer feedback recorded verbatim with permission; revision changes a decision or explains why evidence supports keeping it.

Meet every criterion independently before rating clean. Log your output and corrections.

### Marketing research

Customer interviews, observation, surveys, segmentation and evidence synthesis. Bring your foundations brief.

Preparation: Marketing foundations

- [Nielsen Norman Group · Interview sampling](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/interview-sample-size/) — Recruitment → interviews → surveys: read about sampling and saturation before writing your screener. Practise on real past decisions; do not treat a few interviews as market percentages.
- [Nielsen Norman Group · Usability testing](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/usability-testing-101/) — Observation → synthesis: learn task-based observation, then conduct and code a neutral task session.

#### Frame a research decision

Prerequisites: Entry skill.

Research should reduce uncertainty about a decision. Begin with the customer brief and facts-versus-hypotheses exercise from Marketing foundations if these are unfamiliar.

**Practice.** Write one decision, three unknowns and what different answers would make you do. Select interview, observation, survey or experiment for each unknown.

Next repetition: Reframe a vague request such as “understand our audience” into a decision about an offer.

**Standard.** Every unknown changes an action; methods match questions; interviews are not used to estimate market prevalence.

Meet every criterion independently before rating clean. Log your output and corrections.

#### Recruit contrasting customers

Prerequisites: Frame a research decision.

Convenient respondents can hide important differences. Recruitment criteria should be about relevant behaviour, not flattering opinions.

**Practice.** Draft a screener and recruitment plan including recent buyers, non-buyers and churned users. State exclusions, consent procedure and likely sampling bias.

Next repetition: Design a plan for a hard-to-reach B2B buying group.

**Standard.** Criteria are observable; at least three contrasting cases; no claim the sample represents an entire market.

Meet every criterion independently before rating clean. Log your output and corrections.

#### Ask about a past decision

Prerequisites: Recruit contrasting customers.

Specific past events reveal context and trade-offs. Leading questions and hypothetical purchase promises are weak evidence.

**Practice.** Rewrite ten leading questions. Conduct a 20-minute interview about the last relevant purchase, with permission to take notes. Ask for timeline, alternatives and actual actions.

Next repetition: Interview a lost buyer. Practise neutral follow-ups on contradictions rather than defending your idea.

**Standard.** Questions do not pitch the product; five traceable behavioural observations; quotes and interpretations separated.

Meet every criterion independently before rating clean. Log your output and corrections.

#### Observe a real task

Prerequisites: Recruit contrasting customers.

What people do can differ from what they say. Observe a task without guiding them through the intended solution.

**Practice.** Ask a willing participant to compare or purchase a product in a prototype without payment. Record pauses, workarounds, failures and exact task outcome.

Next repetition: Use a new participant and a competing experience, keeping the task comparable.

**Standard.** Task is realistic; no coaching; observations distinguish action from guessed emotion.

Meet every criterion independently before rating clean. Log your output and corrections.

#### Code evidence into themes

Prerequisites: Ask about a past decision; Observe a real task.

A theme should be traceable to evidence and should retain disconfirming examples.

**Practice.** Code 20 excerpts into triggers, alternatives, barriers and outcomes. Write definitions for each code; ask another reader to independently code five excerpts and resolve disagreements.

Next repetition: Apply the codebook to a fresh interview; revise a category only with an explicit reason.

**Standard.** All excerpts retain source IDs; code definitions distinguish categories; contradictory evidence is included.

Meet every criterion independently before rating clean. Log your output and corrections.

#### Build an unbiased survey

Prerequisites: Code evidence into themes.

A survey needs comprehensible items, sensible answer options and a sampling plan. Small convenience samples do not establish population percentages.

**Practice.** Write six questions for one unresolved theme. Remove double-barrelled wording, add appropriate no-answer options, and pilot with three people who explain each question aloud.

Next repetition: Reverse the order of answer options and identify possible order effects before a new pilot.

**Standard.** Each question measures one thing; response options work; pilot misunderstandings lead to edits; prevalence claims match sampling limits.

Meet every criterion independently before rating clean. Log your output and corrections.

#### Form actionable segments

Prerequisites: Code evidence into themes; Build an unbiased survey.

Useful segments differ in needs or behaviour and imply different actions. Demographic descriptions alone may not change a decision.

**Practice.** Propose three segments from your evidence. For each list trigger, alternative, value sought, reachable channel and uncertainty. Predict one meaningful difference before collecting more evidence.

Next repetition: Test a segment boundary against five new observations and record exceptions.

**Standard.** Segments lead to different decisions; evidence links exist; size and willingness-to-pay guesses are labelled.

Meet every criterion independently before rating clean. Log your output and corrections.

#### Deliver a research decision memo

Prerequisites: Form actionable segments.

Research is useful when its evidence changes a decision while preserving uncertainty.

**Practice.** For a new category, combine interviews or public behavioural evidence with one observation. Deliver a two-page recommendation, evidence appendix, contradictions and next research question for external review.

Next repetition: Return after a real business decision and document which prediction held or failed.

**Standard.** Reader can trace three recommendations to evidence; no fabricated interviews; limitations stated; reviewer challenges addressed.

Meet every criterion independently before rating clean. Log your output and corrections.

### Positioning & go-to-market

Competitive alternatives, segmentation, positioning, launch and sales enablement.

Preparation: Marketing research

- [April Dunford · Introduction to positioning](https://www.aprildunford.com/post/an-introduction-to-positioning) — Alternatives → value → target → positioning: apply the five components to your own evidence, then test whether a buyer understands the result.
- [HubSpot Academy · Inbound Sales](https://academy.hubspot.com/courses/inbound-sales) — Message → enablement: study exploration and advice, then role-play discovery and a buying decision.
- [MIT OpenCourseWare · Marketing Management](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/15-810-marketing-management-fall-2010/pages/syllabus/) — Launch → capstone: connect the positioning choice to product, channel and economics.

#### Map competitive alternatives

Prerequisites: Entry skill.

Positioning starts with what buyers would use without you, including manual work or doing nothing. Use the research tree if you cannot support these alternatives.

**Practice.** For a real product list five alternatives, the circumstances each wins, switching costs and sources. Include the status quo.

Next repetition: Repeat for a service with no obvious direct competitor.

**Standard.** Alternatives reflect customer choices, not only similar products; every claimed advantage has evidence.

Meet every criterion independently before rating clean. Log your output and corrections.

#### Prove differentiated value

Prerequisites: Map competitive alternatives.

A capability matters when it produces a valued outcome that alternatives cannot provide as well in a relevant situation.

**Practice.** Build six capability → mechanism → outcome → evidence chains. Cross out unsupported uniqueness. Pick two that matter to a specific buyer.

Next repetition: Ask a sceptical buyer which chain is irrelevant and revise it.

**Standard.** Mechanism explains the benefit; evidence supports relative claims; buyer relevance is documented.

Meet every criterion independently before rating clean. Log your output and corrections.

#### Select an initial market

Prerequisites: Prove differentiated value.

An initial target focuses execution; it is not a claim that other buyers never matter.

**Practice.** Compare three candidate markets on urgency, ability to pay, access, alternatives and delivery fit. Estimate size with a bottom-up range, not a single impressive TAM number.

Next repetition: Stress-test the choice with acquisition cost doubled and a longer buying cycle.

**Standard.** Scoring is explained; estimates have sources and ranges; choice changes the product or route to market.

Meet every criterion independently before rating clean. Log your output and corrections.

#### Write and test positioning

Prerequisites: Select an initial market.

Connect alternatives, differentiated value, best-fit customers and market category. A positioning statement is an internal choice, not automatically a headline.

**Practice.** Write a one-page positioning document and two category frames. Ask three relevant people to explain what it is, for whom and why choose it.

Next repetition: Apply the same framework to a new category without copying the language.

**Standard.** Responses recorded without coaching; frame reduces confusion; no unsupported “best” claim.

Meet every criterion independently before rating clean. Log your output and corrections.

#### Build a message hierarchy

Prerequisites: Write and test positioning.

Different stakeholders need different evidence while the core promise stays coherent.

**Practice.** Write a core promise, three supporting messages and evidence for user, economic buyer and technical evaluator. Turn one into a five-slide sales narrative.

Next repetition: Present aloud and revise the first question your listener could not answer.

**Standard.** Messages agree; each stakeholder concern has proof; narrative explains the status quo and change.

Meet every criterion independently before rating clean. Log your output and corrections.

#### Design a launch sequence

Prerequisites: Build a message hierarchy.

A launch coordinates product readiness, distribution, sales and measurement; a posting calendar alone is insufficient.

**Practice.** Draft prelaunch, launch and follow-through milestones with owner, dependency, readiness gate and rollback condition. Include customer feedback and operational capacity.

Next repetition: Replan for a two-week delay and half the budget.

**Standard.** Critical dependencies have owners; customer path is complete; success and stop conditions are measurable.

Meet every criterion independently before rating clean. Log your output and corrections.

#### Build sales enablement

Prerequisites: Build a message hierarchy.

A good sales asset helps a buyer make a decision and a seller diagnose fit.

**Practice.** Create a discovery guide, comparison sheet and objection card. Run a role-play with a sceptical buyer; capture where proof or qualification fails.

Next repetition: Repeat with procurement and with a user who lacks buying authority.

**Standard.** Comparison is factual and dated; guide asks before pitching; next action and disqualifiers are clear.

Meet every criterion independently before rating clean. Log your output and corrections.

#### Defend a go-to-market plan

Prerequisites: Design a launch sequence; Build sales enablement.

Integrate research, economics and positioning into a plan that can survive contrary evidence.

**Practice.** Use a second business to present target, positioning, channel, launch, economics and learning plan to an operator. Document assumptions they reject and revise.

Next repetition: Review after a pilot and compare actual demand and conversion with the forecast.

**Standard.** Six decisions connect; cost and capacity feasible; external critique changes the plan; success is not defined by impressions alone.

Meet every criterion independently before rating clean. Log your output and corrections.

### Brand & consumer behaviour

Buying situations, memory, distinctive assets, reach, distribution and brand measurement.

Preparation: Marketing research

- [Ehrenberg-Bass · How B2B Brands Grow](https://sponsors.marketingscience.info/wp-content/uploads/sites/2/reports/2023/11/How-B2B-brands-grow.pdf) — Situations → memory → reach: read the sections on category entry points and growth. Make a falsifiable category diagnosis rather than copying a universal prescription.
- [Ehrenberg-Bass · Measuring brand growth](https://marketingscience.info/how-do-you-measure-how-brands-grow/) — Assets → scorecard: distinguish a useful brand measure from an appealing design judgement.
- [Ehrenberg-Bass · Easy to Find](https://marketingscience.info/news-and-insights/easy-to-find-being-where-b2b-buying-happens) — Access → capstone: inspect where buyers can actually find and buy the offer.

#### Map buying situations

Prerequisites: Entry skill.

Brands can become easier to recall in relevant buying situations. A claimed psychological bias is not proof it drives this purchase.

**Practice.** Collect ten sourced buying situations for one category. Separate occasion, motivation, location and competing alternatives.

Next repetition: Compare habitual low-involvement buying with a high-stakes B2B purchase.

**Standard.** Situations are specific and supported; no invented universal consumer law.

Meet every criterion independently before rating clean. Log your output and corrections.

#### Diagnose mental availability

Prerequisites: Map buying situations.

Recognition, recall in a buying situation and purchase are distinct outcomes. Brand-memory frameworks are hypotheses to measure in the category.

**Practice.** Design an unaided recall exercise for three buying situations, followed by recognition. Pilot the wording; document contamination from showing brand names too early.

Next repetition: Use a new category and separate current customers from non-customers.

**Standard.** Unaided questions precede brand exposure; measures and sampling limits clear; recall is not equated with sales.

Meet every criterion independently before rating clean. Log your output and corrections.

#### Audit distinctive assets

Prerequisites: Diagnose mental availability.

An asset helps identification when buyers associate it with the brand rather than competitors. Looking attractive is a different test.

**Practice.** Inventory colours, shapes, sounds, characters and verbal cues. Design a masked identification task and pilot it with five people as exploratory feedback.

Next repetition: Remove the logo from a second asset set and compare recognition errors.

**Standard.** Competing brand guesses retained; familiarity and uniqueness distinguished; pilot is not claimed as population validation.

Meet every criterion independently before rating clean. Log your output and corrections.

#### Plan reach and frequency

Prerequisites: Map buying situations.

Repeated exposure to the same people is different from reaching more category buyers. Frequency has costs and context-dependent effects.

**Practice.** Compare two plans with the same impressions but different unique reach and frequency. Specify category coverage, placement context and saturation uncertainty.

Next repetition: Add overlapping channels and show why their reach cannot simply be added.

**Standard.** Reach is deduplicated or explicitly estimated; frequency calculation correct; no universal magic frequency assumed.

Meet every criterion independently before rating clean. Log your output and corrections.

#### Audit physical availability

Prerequisites: Map buying situations.

Demand can fail to convert when a product is difficult to find, buy, receive or use.

**Practice.** Trace five buying journeys across search, store or marketplace, checkout and fulfilment. Record availability, access friction and missing options.

Next repetition: Audit a location-based service and an online subscription.

**Standard.** Each issue is observed; fixes have owners; distribution constraints separated from communication problems.

Meet every criterion independently before rating clean. Log your output and corrections.

#### Create a consistent brand system

Prerequisites: Audit distinctive assets; Audit physical availability.

A brand system makes identification consistent across touchpoints while keeping each execution useful.

**Practice.** Create a one-page asset guide and apply it to an ad, landing page, email and package or invoice. Check identification and legibility with another person.

Next repetition: Adapt to a new format without adding unrelated styling.

**Standard.** Core assets stay recognisable; required information survives small sizes; feedback records mistaken identification.

Meet every criterion independently before rating clean. Log your output and corrections.

#### Design a brand scorecard

Prerequisites: Plan reach and frequency; Create a consistent brand system.

A long-term scorecard needs measures connected to buying behaviour, with time horizons and competing explanations.

**Practice.** Specify recall, asset recognition, category penetration and distribution measures. Name sample, cadence, baseline and decision triggered by each.

Next repetition: Explain a case where brand indicators improve while sales fall because distribution shrinks.

**Standard.** Metrics have denominators; change can be distinguished from sample noise; short-term clicks do not substitute for all brand outcomes.

Meet every criterion independently before rating clean. Log your output and corrections.

#### Resolve a brand growth case

Prerequisites: Design a brand scorecard.

Use mental and physical availability as diagnostic lenses, not as a substitute for category evidence.

**Practice.** Write a fresh case diagnosis integrating buying situations, reach, assets and distribution. Have a marketer challenge it; identify one evidence-based intervention and measurement plan.

Next repetition: Compare a small challenger and a mature brand; explain which priorities differ.

**Standard.** Both demand and access assessed; external critique saved; proposed effect is testable with a realistic time horizon.

Meet every criterion independently before rating clean. Log your output and corrections.

### Offers & pricing

Value, unit economics, packaging, price research, elasticity and promotion decisions.

Preparation: Marketing foundations → Marketing research

- [MIT · Pricing lecture](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/15-810-marketing-management-analytics-frameworks-and-applications-fall-2015/af93b992c0c358d2957cc8be6710dedb_MIT15_810F15_L10_Pricing.pdf) — Costs → value → packaging → elasticity: work the numerical cases, close the slides and solve a new case. The old examples teach economics, not current market prices.
- [Kohavi et al. · Online Controlled Experiments and A/B Tests](https://exp-platform.com/Documents/2023-03-11EncyclopeiaMLDSABTestingFinal.pdf) — Promotion → offer test: use randomised comparison principles before interpreting a pricing result.

#### Build a unit-economics model

Prerequisites: Entry skill.

Start with Marketing foundations arithmetic. Separate fixed, variable and acquisition costs before choosing an offer.

**Practice.** Create a sheet for a $120 product with $45 variable cost, $15 expected returns/support per sale and $30 CAC. Calculate contribution before and after acquisition, then break-even orders for $3,000 fixed cost.

Next repetition: Repeat for a monthly service including churn and support capacity.

**Standard.** Answers: $60, $30, 100 orders; assumptions visible; formulas update when inputs change.

Meet every criterion independently before rating clean. Log your output and corrections.

#### Estimate customer value

Prerequisites: Build a unit-economics model.

Value depends on the alternative and the buyer’s circumstances. Willingness to pay is not the same as value claimed by the seller.

**Practice.** Compare time, money, risk and effort for three alternatives. Build a customer value range with explicit assumptions and identify the most uncertain input.

Next repetition: Use a buyer for whom your strongest benefit has no value.

**Standard.** Alternative costs traceable; no double counting benefits; value estimate is not presented as validated willingness to pay.

Meet every criterion independently before rating clean. Log your output and corrections.

#### Design coherent packages

Prerequisites: Estimate customer value.

Packages should map to real differences in need or usage rather than arbitrary feature withholding.

**Practice.** Design three packages with unit of value, inclusions, boundaries, service cost and migration path. Explain who should not buy each.

Next repetition: Remove a feature and predict who changes tier and why.

**Standard.** Every tier has an economic and customer rationale; terms understandable; margins calculated.

Meet every criterion independently before rating clean. Log your output and corrections.

#### Investigate price sensitivity

Prerequisites: Design coherent packages.

Stated preferences can diverge from behaviour. Price research needs realistic alternatives and careful interpretation.

**Practice.** Write a neutral price interview and a choice exercise with realistic alternatives. Pilot with three people; separate affordability, fairness and value objections.

Next repetition: Change the context or alternative and examine how answers move.

**Standard.** Questions do not lead; choices include no purchase; sample limitations and hypothetical bias explicit.

Meet every criterion independently before rating clean. Log your output and corrections.

#### Model a price change

Prerequisites: Investigate price sensitivity.

Price affects both margin and demand; a revenue increase can conceal a profit decline.

**Practice.** At price $100, cost $60 and 100 units, compare price $90 with 120 units, and price $110 with 90 units. Calculate revenue and contribution before fixed costs.

Next repetition: Calculate the volume required for a 10% discount to preserve contribution, then test other margins.

**Standard.** Baseline $10,000/$4,000; discount $10,800/$3,600; increase $9,900/$4,500. Volume assumptions are predictions, not facts.

Meet every criterion independently before rating clean. Log your output and corrections.

#### Evaluate a promotion

Prerequisites: Model a price change.

A promotion may shift purchase timing or subsidise existing demand. Evaluate incremental contribution and operational effects.

**Practice.** Model a promotion including cannibalisation, pull-forward, returns, fulfilment and new buyers. Compare against a no-promotion scenario and a non-price offer.

Next repetition: Repeat for a subscription trial with low conversion and high service costs.

**Standard.** All incremental costs included; optimistic and pessimistic cases shown; revenue alone does not determine the winner.

Meet every criterion independently before rating clean. Log your output and corrections.

#### Design an offer test

Prerequisites: Evaluate a promotion.

A test needs consistent terms, suitable allocation, economics and a predeclared decision rule.

**Practice.** Write an offer-test brief: population, variants, allocation, primary outcome, margin guardrail, run length and inconclusive rule. Use a simulated dataset if no real pilot exists.

Next repetition: Compare a packaging change against a price-only change.

**Standard.** Offer and price changes are disentangled or interpretation narrowed; no unapproved spend; analysis matches allocation.

Meet every criterion independently before rating clean. Log your output and corrections.

#### Recommend a pricing decision

Prerequisites: Design an offer test.

Combine customer value and economics without overstating what small samples can establish.

**Practice.** Present a new product’s package and pricing recommendation, model, evidence gaps and experiment to an operator. Revise after their cost and demand objections.

Next repetition: Revisit after customer behaviour is observed and explain forecast errors.

**Standard.** Model reconciles; evidence supports chosen range; sensitivity and external review included.

Meet every criterion independently before rating clean. Log your output and corrections.

### Copywriting

Write persuasive copy from customer evidence. Build a portfolio one short, assessed exercise at a time.

Preparation: None; use the entry exercise.

- [Copy That! — copywriting lessons](https://www.copythatshow.com/) — Use a lesson to repair a specific weakness, then write your own version from a fresh brief.

#### Write a one-reader brief

Prerequisites: Entry skill.

A useful brief identifies one reader, their situation, the offer and one desired action. Use a real product throughout this tree.

**Practice.** In 10 minutes, write a 100-word brief for one product: reader, trigger, problem, current alternative and action. Mark assumptions.

**Standard.** One specific reader and action; a concrete problem; assumptions explicitly labelled.

#### Collect customer language

Prerequisites: Write a one-reader brief.

Customer language supplies raw material. Separate verbatim observations from your interpretation; never invent testimonials.

**Practice.** Collect five public reviews or interview excerpts. Record source, situation, pain, desired outcome and exact wording.

**Standard.** Five traceable excerpts; each has a situation and outcome; no invented quotes.

#### Translate features into benefits

Prerequisites: Write a one-reader brief.

A feature matters through what it lets this reader do. Avoid benefits that any competitor could claim.

**Practice.** Write ten feature → mechanism → reader outcome chains. Cut unsupported promises and keep the three most specific.

**Standard.** Each chain explains how the feature creates the outcome; no unsupported guarantee.

#### Build a credible offer

Prerequisites: Collect customer language; Translate features into benefits.

An offer connects an outcome, deliverable, terms and credible proof. Urgency must have a real reason.

**Practice.** Write a 120-word offer with deliverables, price placeholder, proof, constraints and a single next step.

**Standard.** Reader can identify what they get and what they must do; claims have evidence or are removed.

#### Write twenty distinct headlines

Prerequisites: Build a credible offer.

Headlines select a reader and make the next sentence worth reading. Different words around the same idea are not different angles.

**Practice.** Write twenty headlines in 20 minutes across benefit, problem, mechanism and objection angles. Select three and explain the trade-offs.

**Standard.** Twenty drafts; at least four angles; chosen headlines are specific, truthful and relevant to the brief.

#### Write a lead that earns the next line

Prerequisites: Write twenty distinct headlines.

The lead connects the headline to a problem the reader recognises, then introduces a plausible route forward.

**Practice.** Write three 80-word leads for the same headline. Use a concrete situation, problem or demonstration. Read aloud and cut throat-clearing.

**Standard.** Headline promise is continued; reader recognises the situation; no claim outruns evidence.

#### Answer objections with proof

Prerequisites: Build a credible offer.

Proof answers a particular doubt. A demonstration, measured result or specific example is stronger than an unsupported adjective.

**Practice.** List five objections. Pair each with available evidence; write a 50-word response. Label evidence still needed.

**Standard.** All five objections are answered honestly; missing evidence is visible; limitations are preserved.

#### Write a clear call to action

Prerequisites: Answer objections with proof.

The call to action tells the reader exactly what happens next and removes avoidable uncertainty.

**Practice.** Write five calls to action including button text and adjacent reassurance for your offer.

**Standard.** One action per version; next step and commitment are clear; reassurance is factual.

#### Assemble a landing page

Prerequisites: Write a lead that earns the next line; Answer objections with proof; Write a clear call to action.

Sequence information in the order a sceptical reader needs it: relevance, value, proof, concerns, action.

**Practice.** Draft a 500-word landing page from a blank page. Include headline, lead, benefits, proof, objections and CTA.

**Standard.** All six parts serve one offer; no contradictions; reader can explain offer and action.

#### Edit against a fixed rubric

Prerequisites: Assemble a landing page.

Editing is a separate repetition: locate a defect, name why it hurts the reader, revise and compare.

**Practice.** Cut your page by 20% without losing evidence or terms. Keep before/after versions and annotate five meaningful edits.

**Standard.** Five edits improve clarity or credibility; required information survives; no new unsupported claims.

#### Test copy on a new offer

Prerequisites: Edit against a fixed rubric.

Transfer means writing for a different reader without copying your first page. Comprehension evidence is not proof of conversion lift.

**Practice.** Write a page for a second offer. Ask three readers to explain it and its next step without coaching. Revise their largest misunderstanding.

**Standard.** Three recorded reader responses; an independent draft; one revision tied to observed confusion.

#### Define an ad brief

Prerequisites: Entry skill.

**Practice.** Pick a real offer. Write audience, problem, promise, proof, destination and one conversion event in 100 words.

**Standard.** All six are explicit and consistent; success is an action rather than a vague awareness goal.

Prerequisites: Define an ad brief.

**Practice.** Collect five ads. Annotate hook, audience cue, promise, proof, visual, CTA and what you cannot know about results.

**Standard.** Five complete breakdowns; performance claims are not inferred from visibility.

#### Generate distinct creative angles

Prerequisites: Deconstruct five ads.

An angle changes the reason someone should care: problem, benefit, demonstration, comparison or objection.

**Practice.** Write twelve concepts across four angles for the same offer. Each gets a hook, proof idea and CTA.

**Standard.** Four meaningfully different reasons to care; each supported by plausible, honest proof.

#### Produce hooks for one angle

Prerequisites: Generate distinct creative angles.

The opening must make the content relevant quickly and deliver on its promise.

**Practice.** Write ten opening lines and sketch ten first frames. Pair the best three and explain who each selects.

**Standard.** First frame and line reinforce each other; product relevance is clear; no misleading bait.

#### Design a readable static ad

Prerequisites: Produce hooks for one angle.

Hierarchy directs attention to one message. Inspect the actual mobile size, not only a large canvas.

**Practice.** Make three static ads with different layouts. View each on your phone for five seconds and test message recall with another person.

**Standard.** Offer and CTA are legible; imagery supports the message; tester identifies intended message.

#### Write a short video script

Prerequisites: Produce hooks for one angle.

A usable script specifies both what is seen and what is heard, including a demonstration and next step.

**Practice.** Write three 20–30 second scripts: problem/solution, demonstration and objection. Time a spoken read.

**Standard.** All fit the target duration; visual directions are shootable; proof and CTA are included.

#### Capture usable footage

Prerequisites: Write a short video script.

Clear audio, adequate light and relevant demonstration footage matter more than elaborate production.

**Practice.** Record one script plus five supporting shots. Listen with headphones and inspect focus before editing.

**Standard.** Speech intelligible; subject in focus; all claims supported by what is shown or stated.

#### Edit a complete video ad

Prerequisites: Capture usable footage.

Every shot must move the message forward. Captions should make sense with sound off.

**Practice.** Cut a 20–30 second ad with captions and CTA. Watch once without sound and once at normal phone size.

**Standard.** Captions accurate and readable; no dead opening; demonstration and CTA survive both checks.

#### Make a controlled variation set

Prerequisites: Design a readable static ad; Edit a complete video ad.

To learn why a change matters, hold the offer, audience and destination steady while changing one creative variable.

**Practice.** Create three versions varying only the hook. Name the hypothesis for each and inventory what is held constant.

**Standard.** Versions differ only in the declared variable; each prediction can be contradicted by results.

#### Read campaign arithmetic

Prerequisites: Define an ad brief.

Clicks, conversions and revenue answer different questions. A cheap click can still produce expensive customers.

**Practice.** For 10,000 impressions, 200 clicks, 8 purchases, $160 spend and $400 revenue, calculate CTR, CPC, conversion rate, CPA and ROAS. Repeat with new numbers.

**Standard.** Answers: 2%, $0.80, 4%, $20 and 2.5×. Denominators correct; ROAS is not profit.

#### Design a creative experiment

Prerequisites: Make a controlled variation set; Read campaign arithmetic.

Predeclare the decision and avoid calling a winner from a handful of conversions. Use simulation if no campaign budget is authorised.

**Practice.** Write a test plan with one variable, allocation, primary metric, duration, spend ceiling and an inconclusive outcome. Analyse a mock result.

**Standard.** Plan distinguishes a useful signal from noise; inconclusive results are allowed; no spend required.

#### Deliver a creative learning report

Prerequisites: Design a creative experiment.

A portfolio should show the reasoning, finished assets and what the next iteration will test.

**Practice.** Package one static ad, one video, three hook variants and a one-page result report using real or clearly labelled simulated data.

**Standard.** Assets share one brief; report distinguishes observation from inference; next change addresses evidence.

#### Quantify uncertainty

#### Interpret an experimental result

Statistical evidence, practical value and instrumentation validity are separate checks.

**Practice.** Analyse control 200/2,000 and treatment 240/2,000. Calculate absolute and relative lift, rough difference interval, and a decision given a 3-point minimum useful effect.

Next repetition: Repeat with a negative result and with an allocation mismatch that invalidates a simple readout.

Distribution choices change access, margin, control and partner incentives. They are marketing decisions, not just logistics.

**Practice.** Compare direct, marketplace, reseller and physical distribution for one offer. Model margins, coverage, conflict and customer experience.

Next repetition: Adapt to a new geography using local evidence and a qualified reviewer rather than literal translation.

**Standard.** Full channel costs included; incentives considered; access benefit supported; conflicts have an operating response.

#### Manage creative and analytical work

Prerequisites: Allocate a constrained budget; Design a route to market.

Management improves output through clear briefs, useful feedback, realistic capacity and accountability.

**Practice.** Write a team plan with responsibilities, milestones and quality rubrics. Review one weak creative asset and one weak analysis: identify the defect, evidence and next action.

Next repetition: Role-play disagreement between a creative lead and analyst and document a testable resolution.

**Standard.** Feedback actionable; no taste-only judgement presented as fact; workload feasible; decision owner clear.

Meet every criterion independently before rating clean. Log your output and corrections.

#### Defend a portfolio of decisions

Prerequisites: Manage creative and analytical work.

A professional portfolio should include unsuccessful work and explain decisions using the evidence available at the time.

**Practice.** Assemble three category case studies with brief, original output, economics, feedback, revisions and results. Ask an experienced marketer to challenge the weakest case.

Next repetition: Remove your most polished case and defend the remaining two.

**Standard.** Results distinguished from simulations; contribution claims proportional to evidence; recurring weaknesses translated into drills.

Meet every criterion independently before rating clean. Log your output and corrections.

#### Run a complete field learning cycle

Prerequisites: Defend a portfolio of decisions.

Elite performance requires sustained external results and judgement; completing a curriculum cannot certify a percentile.

**Practice.** With an authorised real business, predeclare a bounded intervention and measurement plan, execute, collect outcomes and write a postmortem with the owner. If access is unavailable, do a labelled simulation and leave field validation outstanding.

Next repetition: Repeat across several business cycles and categories, increasing responsibility only as evidence supports it.

**Standard.** Owner confirms execution; costs and outcomes documented; uncertainty and counterfactual limits explicit; independent review completed; next practice target follows the largest error.
